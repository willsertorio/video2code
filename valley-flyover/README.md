# Valley Flyover

A 19.3-second drone clip over forested hills (1920 x 1080, 24 fps, 464 frames), turned into code twice: by Gemini 4 Carbon from one prompt, and by hand as a 3D scene rebuilt from the footage.

- `player.html`: the original clip, Gemini 4 Carbon's version and the Human version, locked to the same frame. Play or pause with the button or Space; step with the arrow keys; Shift + arrows jumps ten frames.
- `carbon.html`: Gemini 4 Carbon's answer, unchanged (35 KB). The prompt, sent with a 1280 x 720 copy of the clip, was "transform this video into code, be the more realistic as you can". It is a landscape generated by a shader, 18.5 s long. `carbon_response.json` is the raw reply; `grading/generate_carbon.py` is the script that asked.
- `human.html`: the Human version on its own. Add `?f=<frame>` (0-463) to freeze on one source frame.
- `scene.js`: the measured scene and the renderer behind the Human version (about 28 MB).
- `ref.mp4`: a 1280 x 720 copy of the source clip, used only by the player.

All pages open straight from disk and load nothing from the network.

## How the Human version works

The clip is a camera flying forward through a scene where nothing else moves, so the whole clip can be described as a fixed 3D scene plus a camera path:

- **Keyframes**: 78 stills taken from the footage, one every 6 frames, each with a depth grid (483 x 273 points) recovered from how the picture shifts between neighbouring views.
- **Camera path**: the drone's position and direction for all 464 frames, plus the lens's focal length and distortion.
- **Wobble correction**: a 32 x 18 grid of sub-pixel shifts per frame, for motion in the source that a rigid camera cannot follow.
- **Sharpening**: one amount per frame, so keyframes and the frames between them carry the same fine detail.

Each keyframe becomes a textured 3D surface. For every frame, WebGL draws the nearest surfaces through the recovered camera; where something in front has moved aside, the stretched part of a surface is cut away and a neighbouring keyframe shows through. One frame in six is a stored still seen from its own position; the other five are drawn from the geometry.

Measured against the source over all 464 frames at 1920 x 1080: 39.5 dB PSNR on average (worst frame 37.1), SSIM 0.978, colour bias under 0.2 levels.

Known differences: between keyframes, mostly in the last 5 seconds at the lower right, the outline of near trees against bright roofs shows small specks and jagged fragments, and near foliage is slightly softer than on keyframes. Frame rate on a real GPU has not been measured.

## Rubric and scores

- `rubric.json`: 40 criteria written for this clip (3 gates and 37 scored), following the framework's dimensions: Builds, Playback, Prompt adherence, Reference fidelity, Scene fidelity, Composition, Lighting, Materials, Motion, Realism, Camera path, Viewpoint consistency and Motion timing. The Orbit gate is left out.
- `rubric_carbon.json`: the criteria scored on both versions by `google/gemini-4-carbon` through OpenRouter. Fields `m` and `mc` are the verdict and comment on Gemini 4 Carbon's version, `r` and `rc` on the Human version.

| Version | Pass | Fail | Unverified |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gemini 4 Carbon | 12 | 27 | 1 |
| Human | 35 | 4 | 1 |

- `grading/carbon/` and `grading/human/`: what the grader was sent for each version (`page.mp4`, six paired stills, four close-ups, `notes.txt`) and its raw reply (`response.json`). `grading/reference.mp4` is the reference it was sent with both.
- `grading/grade.py` sends it; `grading/make_evidence.py` builds the stills, close-ups and capture video from page screenshots; `grading/build_sample_page.py` writes the viewer page `valley-flyover.html` at the repository root; `grading/build_player.py` writes `player.html` from `grading/player_shell.html`.

Read the scores with these limits in mind:

- Gemini 4 Carbon graded its own answer as well as the Human version.
- It could not run the pages. It judged captures and stills, and took the build, playback and frame-rate facts from each `notes.txt`. The 30 fps gate is unverified for both.
- Carbon's version was captured at every third frame and 320 x 180 for the motion capture (stills at 960 x 540), because its shader is very slow without a GPU. The Human version was captured at every frame.
- One generation and one grading run per version. An earlier run on the Human version, with a differently worded first criterion and notes that listed its known weaknesses, gave 34 pass, 5 fail.

To grade again from this folder:

```bash
export OPENROUTER_API_KEY=...        # your key, never committed
python3 grading/grade.py rubric.json grading/carbon rubric_carbon.json m
python3 grading/grade.py rubric.json grading/human rubric_carbon.json r
python3 grading/build_sample_page.py
```
